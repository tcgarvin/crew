# Ship Life: Implementation Plan

Status: plan, 2026-10-04. Design and rationale: `docs/design/ship-life.md` (section references
below are to that file unless marked Dockside). Eight slices, as §12 (re-sequenced 2026-10-06, D91). Each slice ends deployed,
with all checks green (`pnpm typecheck && pnpm lint && pnpm depcruise && pnpm test`), a phone
playtest, and a pause to take stock. Slices 1–4 are planned in full below; slice 5 was
re-planned around the asteroid field on 2026-10-06 (D91) and slices 6–8 are sequenced after it. Each slice adds its own `D<n>` entries.
Slice 2 was planned on 2026-10-04 after slice 1 landed (D72–D74); slices 3 and 4 were planned
the same day against slice 2's gate and plan, before its workstreams merged, slice 4 also
against slice 3's plan.

Slice 1 is built by one serial *contract gate* followed by parallel workstreams in separate
worktrees. The gate changes every shared type and leaves the repo green; each workstream then owns
a disjoint set of files. Shared files are named with their owner.

## Slice 1: Freighter Mk2, hull contract, berth boxes, bow east

Scope (§12.1): the `hull` block and its invariants; the `hullContract` validator pass; dock maps
on `berth` boxes with the six maps re-authored; the renderer flipped to bow-east; Mk1 mirrored and
kept loadable; the Mk2 deck with every room and piece of furniture placed, mostly inert; berth
assignment and lockers; the ninth crew member. Save schema 6 → 7. Behaviour is otherwise
unchanged: nobody cooks, no watches, no dirt.

### Calls made

Where the design is silent or does not fit the code, the plan decides; each is one line.

1. **`hull` carries no `rampSlots`.** The ramp tiles are already the ramp doors' edges
   (`docking.*RampDoorKey`); a copy would drift. The validator checks the doors instead.
2. **Orientation is checked as: the `bridge` compartment touches the east hull edge, and both
   ramp doors are on side `s`.** The sim has no direction, so "bow east" has to be read off the
   layout.
3. **`DockDef.shipAt` goes; the ship's place is derived.** `berthPlacement(dock, deck)` in
   `core/docks.ts` puts the ship flush to the box's south edge and centred, so every reader is
   found by the compiler and two footprints share one rule.
4. **The Mk1 mirror and the dock-map conversion run in the gate**, not as workstreams: until both
   are done the authored bundle fails the new rules, and every stream's tests load it.
5. **`machine.bridge_console` becomes `machine.helm` now**, `galley` machine tag becomes `stove`,
   and the medbay bed is retagged `medBed` (no longer a sleep target, §7). The design puts the
   helm rename in slice 2; authoring Mk2 against a name that changes next slice is worse.
6. **Berth assignment is a standing rule, not a one-off:** any crew member or passenger without
   a berth gets the first free bed that fits their rank, every tick. That covers scenario start,
   a passenger boarding, a narrator-spawned crew member and a restored save with one line.
7. **Saves older than schema 7 are refused.** Mk1's coordinates are mirrored, and the dock maps
   change shape; migrating every tile-bearing field (locations, paths, plan steps, facts, claims)
   is the "change the compiler cannot find" D59 warned about. `restore` throws
   `SaveCompatibilityError`; the host already starts a new game on that path
   (`apps/web/src/worker/host.ts`). PLAN §3 allows it before players exist.
8. **Lockers link to beds by `ZoneDef.machineKey`** (new, optional: the placement a zone serves),
   not by key convention. Personal items (`personal` item tag) in a crew member's starting
   inventory are set down in their berth's locker at scenario start; `Item.ownerId` records
   whose they are. Nobody fetches them until slice 4.
9. **Starting stores stay in `cargo_stow`.** The pantry, parts and cleaning-locker zones are
   placed but unused: crew learn item locations by looking, and nothing walks through Stores yet.
   Slice 3 moves provisions and parts there with the cook loop.
10. **`rampLandings` overrides are not built** (§10 says none of the six need them).
11. **The ninth crew member is Noor Haddad**, role `pilot`, piloting 3, shift `beta` (so one
    piloting ≥3 is on every shift: Mara and Jax alpha, Dane and Noor beta). The design uses the
    name "Noor" in its own examples.
12. **`crew content render-dock` composites every deck in the bundle**, one map per deck, so the
    README shows both footprints in each box.
13. **Dock READMEs are regenerated by the lead at integration**; no stream hand-edits them, and a
    stream whose change alters them (a new deck) may skip the staleness test locally.
14. **The two reserved bridge tiles are floor with nothing on them**, named in the deck README;
    stations arrive as content in slice 5.
15. **`grime` and `airborneDust` wait for slice 3.**
16. **The narrator prompt's "eight people" becomes "nine"** (prompt version bump); the lead runs
    `pnpm test:live` once at the end of integration.

### Gate: contracts (one agent, serial)

Everything below lands as one commit with all four checks green. Stubs are fine where behaviour
is a workstream's; types, schemas, generated schemas, scripts and the minimum content to keep the
bundle valid are not.

**Sim types (`packages/sim/src/core/types/content.ts`).**

- `HULL_SIZE_CLASSES = ["S", "M", "L"] as const`, `HullSizeClass`, and
  `HULL_SIZE_BOX: Record<HullSizeClass, { w, h }>` = S 24×14, M 36×20, L 40×24.
- `DeckDef.hull: HullDef` where `HullDef = { class: string; sizeClass: HullSizeClass }`
  (required).
- `ZoneDef.machineKey?: LocalKey`.
- `DockDef.berth: { origin: TileCoord; sizeClass: HullSizeClass }` replaces `shipAt`; the doc
  comment on `DockDef` is rewritten (tile frame, the box, the ship derived).
- `DOCK_TILE_KINDS = ["floor", "wall", "void"]` (`berth` removed).
- `MACHINE_TAGS` gains `navStation`, `opsStation`, `chair`, `desk`, `stove`, `medBed`,
  `messTable` (exists), `gamesTable`, `vidScreen`, `exerciseRig`, `workbench`, `console`, and
  loses `galley`.
- `ITEM_TAGS` gains `personal`.

**Sim state (`core/types/state.ts`, `core/types/ship.ts`, `core/types/view.ts`).**

- `WorldState.berths: Record<ActorId, LocalKey>` (bed placement key; owner: needs). Added to
  `emptyState` in `core/world.ts`.
- `UniqueItem.ownerId?: ActorId`.
- `SelfView.berth?: { machineId: MachineId }` (filled by `view/agentView.ts` from `berths` and the
  machine table; stub returning it is fine).
- `SCHEMA_VERSION = 7`, with the history line. `core/migrations.ts` gains `6:` a migration that
  throws `SaveCompatibilityError("save predates ship life (schema 6); start a new game")`
  (call 7). `core/save.ts` imports nothing new; `save.test.ts` gets one test that a schema-6
  envelope is refused and the host test (`apps/web/src/worker/host.test.ts`) that it starts a new
  game still passes.
- `core/invariants.ts`: `berthViolations` stub (every key is an actor, every value a placement key
  of a `bed`-tagged machine on the deck, no two actors share a value). The content-free
  `checkInvariants` can check keys and uniqueness; the machine check goes in a
  `systems/needs/invariants.ts` the berth stream writes.

**Berth placement (`core/docks.ts`).** `berthPlacement(dock, deck): TileCoord` =
`{ x: origin.x + floor((box.w - deck.width) / 2), y: origin.y + box.h - deck.height }`, throwing
if the deck exceeds its class box or the box its class. `dockToDeck`/`deckToDock`/`isDockFloorAt`
and everything downstream take `{ dock, shipAt }` (a `BerthedDock`) or `(dock, deck)`; the
content-only helpers (`dockForPort`, `dockAlongside`, …) that need the deck widen their `content`
pick to `"docks" | "decks"` and read the deck from `state.deck.dock.deckId`. `systems/spatial/
geometry.ts` (`gridBounds`, `dockFloorTiles`, `dockGraft.offset`), `core/locals.ts`
(`rampLandings`), `core/floor-set.ts`, `systems/travel/*` compile against the new signatures.
`packages/sim/src/index.ts` exports `berthPlacement`, `HULL_SIZE_BOX`.

**The ship's deck is the world's, not the bundle's first.** Three helpers take
`Object.values(content.decks)[0]`: `systems/travel/travel.ts` `theDeckId` (ramps, `attachDock`,
and `systems/commerce/passage.ts:93` through it), `systems/commerce/tasks.ts` `firstDeck` (haul
tiles), `systems/commerce/jobs.ts` `cargoStowageZoneKey`. With two decks they pick whichever
`loader.ts` lists first. Add `shipDeckId(state)` in `core/world.ts` (the one key of
`state.deck.tiles`; throws on none or several, as the migrations and narrator fixtures already
assume) and use it in all three.

**Content schemas (`packages/content/src/schemas/`).** `deck.ts`: `hull`, zone `machineKey`.
`dock.ts`: `berth`, tile kinds. `primitives.ts`: machine and item tag enums (the `AssertEqual`
guards force this). Regenerate `schema-json/*.json` with `crew content schemas`.
`validate-dock.ts`: `checkBerth` becomes the box check in its simplest form (box inside the map,
every box tile `void`, the deck's floor inside the box), `dockRampLandings(dock, deck)` uses
`berthPlacement`; the full rule set is the validator stream's.

**Renderer, compile-only.** `apps/web/src/render/dock.ts` (`dockOrigin`, `dockRectOnDeck`),
`camera.ts`, `dockHit.ts`, `dockShape.ts` (berth box from `dock.berth` and `HULL_SIZE_BOX`,
`runs.berth` gone, `quay` edges = box perimeter edges that meet floor), `dockArt.ts`
(`fillRuns(shape.runs.berth)` becomes a box fill), `DeckCanvas.tsx` and tests, all using
`berthPlacement(dock, deck)` from the layout's deck. No direction change here.

**CLI.** `render-dock.ts` composites every deck (one map block per deck, "docked at (x,y)" per
deck); `index.ts` drops the "exactly one deck" rule; `index.test.ts` literals follow.

**Scripts (one-off, kept under `scripts/`).**

- `scripts/mirror-deck.mts <deck.json>`: `x → width-1-x` for every tile row, door and
  opening/wall edge (side `e ↔ w`), zone tile, spawn and transfer tile; machines are re-placed
  from their mirrored absolute footprint: a def whose footprint and interaction offsets are
  symmetric in x keeps its rotation, otherwise rotation 180 (which also flips y offsets; the
  validator decides whether the result is on floor). Runs on `freighter-mk1.json`; the README is
  regenerated by `crew content render-deck` and its hand-written header says east is fore.
- `scripts/convert-dock-berth.mts <dock.json>`: `berth` legend rows become `void`; `berth:
  { origin, sizeClass: "M" }` is written so that `berthPlacement` for the 32×18 Mk1 lands where
  `shipAt` was (origin = shipAt − (2, 2)); where that puts the box above row 0, void rows are
  inserted just above the ship (hub, Kessler, Ossory, Vesper: 2; Tern's Rest: 1; Green Meridian:
  0) and every post, area, prop and foreground rect at or below the insertion shifts or grows;
  every tile inside the box becomes void and a prop inside it is dropped with a log line. Tern's
  Rest keeps its width (origin x 0, so the ship moves one tile east). Then `crew content
  render-dock` for each.

**Content, minimum to stay valid.** Mk1 (mirrored) gets `hull: { class: "light_freighter",
sizeClass: "M" }`; `machine.bridge_console` is renamed `machine.helm` (file `helm.json`, placement
key `helm_1`, a second interaction position `standby` for §2, `use`); `galley_stove.json` tag
`stove`; `medbay_bed.json` tag `medBed`; two new inert defs, `nav-terminal.json` (`navStation`)
and `mess-table.json` (`messTable`, footprint 2×1, four `seat` positions, `use`), each placed
once on Mk1 (bridge and mess) so the hull contract the validator stream enforces already holds.
Hand-built sim fixtures with a `berth` legend (`systems/spatial/fixtures/dock.ts`,
`systems/work/fixtures/crew-deck.ts`, and the travel/commerce `fixtures-dock.ts` files) get the
same conversion by hand.

**Tests the gate must fix to stay green** (literals only; the rules stay): `apps/cli/src/
index.test.ts` (hub "docked at", `personnel_ramp: (5,19)` → the mirrored landing, `decks[0]`,
and the flood test, which must flood the box rows with floor instead of relegending `B`);
`packages/content/src/schemas/validate-dock.test.ts` (`PERSONNEL_LANDING`/`CARGO_LANDING`, the
`shipAt {4,1}` message becomes a `berth.origin` one, the hub's 38×28 literals after the row

## Slice 3: Mess call and dirt

Scope (§12.3, §4, §5): `provisions` and `hot_meal` items; the cook's meal before each bell, eaten
at the mess tables, the oncoming watch first; the helm-stander's plate carried up; spoilage;
ration packs as the fallback; `provisionsLow` and buying provisions at port like parts; a dirt
grid per deck fed by footfall, boots, port grime and dust and the things people do; room tiers
with thoughts; `compartmentDirty` → mopping with the mop from the cleaning locker, by role
affinity, by on-duty crew with nothing better to do; the grime tint, the room inspector, ticker
lines on tier changes; the digest's galley and dirt lines; the beats report as the tuning tool.
Save schema 8 → 9 (migrated). Prompt v0.22 (`pnpm test:live` once). Built on slice 2 as planned
and as its gate notes said it was built (`ship-life-slice2-gate-notes.md`, deleted at slice 2's
integration; read it in git history): sections and the bell,
`helmRoleOf`/`whoHasTheHelm`, `addThought`, `crew beats`, day-relative knobs.

Built as one serial gate commit, then five parallel workstreams, then a tuning commit by the lead.

### Calls made

1. **A hot meal on the counter is what fills the mess, not a hunger timer.** Today eating is
   need-driven (`seekThreshold` 0.5) and solitary. Now the galley serves a stack of `hot_meal`
   on the `serving` zone before each bell and tells every crew member where it is (a `reported`
   item fact, the way a purchase is reported): that is the mess call. The planner's eat candidate
   (`planner/galley.ts`) takes a known hot meal at routine class from
   `galley.mealCallHungerAbove` (0.2, well under `seekThreshold`) when the person is a dayworker,
   off watch, or in the section that stands the next watch (`self.watch.nextOnWatch`, new) before
   the bell; the section on watch waits for the bell and eats as the off-going. Need-driven eating
   stays as it is and prefers a known hot meal to a ration. Nobody is pulled off a routine or
   higher activity for a meal (same-class candidates never interrupt): a hauler finishes the
   crate, a sleeper eats on waking, and the gangway stander's hold ends for the meal call
   (`gangwayHoldDone`) with the gangway candidate suppressed while it stands
   (`mealCallFor(view, config)`, exported from `planner/galley.ts`, read by `planner/helm.ts`).
2. **The meal is a problem the galley owns, not a personal activity.** `galleySystem` raises
   `mealToCook { watchIndex }` at `bell − mealLeadDays` for the coming watch (dedupe
   `meal:<watchIndex>`, public, routine), resolves it when the serve commits, and cancels it with
   `mealMissed` at `bell + mealGraceDays`. It derives one `cookMeal` task at the stove's `use`
   tile with `requiredSkills: [{ cooking, min: 1 }]` and no consumables: the plan is the galley's
   own (`cookPlan`), one crate of provisions split off the nearest known stack, `cookSeconds` of
   `cook` work at the stove (skill-scaled through `workRate`), a step to the serving zone and the
   `serve` commit, which consumes the crate and spawns `crewAboard + spareServings` servings
   (never more than `servingsPerCrate`) on the serving tile. The generic `supplySteps` is not
   used: it carries the whole stack (it does for spare parts today), and eighteen crates of
   provisions walking to the stove is not the picture.
3. **The cook cooks every meal; anyone with cooking ≥ 1 substitutes after a wait.** `cookMeal`
   is exempt from the off-duty rule and `deferTo` for the `cook` role (as helm tasks are for the
   stander, slice 2 call 6): Bettina is a dayworker off during the third watch and still cooks
   it, sleeping in between. Anyone else may claim it only once the problem is
   `galley.substituteAfterDays` old (the cook asleep, ashore, in the infirmary, or just slow).
   The substitute's dinner is as good as their skill. No `cookUnavailable` state: age is the
   signal.
4. **Quality is the cook's skill, lowered a step by a dirty galley, and it rides on the item.**
   `MealQuality = "poor" | "plain" | "good"`: cooking ≥ `goodCookingAbove` (6) good, <
   `poorCookingBelow` (3) poor, else plain; the galley at `dirty` or worse drops it one step.
   `Item.meal = { quality, spoilsAtTick, cookId }` is set at the serve, so a later cook or a
   content retune never rewrites a plate already on the counter. Thoughts on eating
   (`consumeServing`, shared by the eat step and the helm meal): `ateWell` (+0.1, 0.3 day) for
   good, `burntDinner` (−0.1, 0.3 day) for poor, nothing for plain; `ateRationPack` (−0.05, 0.3
   day) for any `food` without a `meal`; `ateInDirtyMess` (−0.1, 0.5 day) and `ateInFilthyMess`
   (−0.2, 0.5 day) by the room the eater is in: two keys rather than a doubled value, because a
   thought's value is copied from its def (D79). Five new `THOUGHT_KEYS`, all non-stacking.
5. **Meals go to the helm, and the stander eats in the seat.** When a meal is served, someone
   holds the helm (`whoHasTheHelm`) and no serving lies within reach of the seat, the galley
   raises `mealToCarry { toActorId, machineId }` → one `carryMeal` task (routine; the cook or a
   dayworker, anyone after `routineAgingDays`): split one serving off the counter, walk to the
   helm's `standby` tile, set it down. The stander never leaves the hold to eat: a `staff` hold
   is essential class and the planner would not let a routine meal interrupt it, and a vacancy
   for a meal is exactly what slice 2's relief and `helmUnattended` would punish. Instead the
   galley runs the meal on the spot: with a serving within reach of the seat-holder and their
   hunger above the call threshold, `galley.helmMeal = { actorId, itemId, startedTick }` counts
   `needs.mealDays` and then `consumeServing` runs (hunger, thought, `ateMeal`, a crumb of dirt);
   the label reads "at the helm, eating". One-handed, like a real watch. `urgency.routineAgingDays`
   is read for the first time, here and in call 11.
6. **Eating happens at a mess seat.** The eat plan's table is a free `use` position of a
   `messTable` machine (eight seats on Mk2), chosen against the reservation cache at plan time
   (truth, as plans may use it), else a floor tile in the mess as today; a seat lost on the way
   (`occupiedPosition`) backs the meal off for `retryBackoffSeconds` and the next plan picks
   another. Someone already in the mess or galley eats where they stand.
7. **Spoilage is time on the item, swept by the galley.** `mealSpoilDays` (0.25, so a serving
   outlives one sleep) after the serve, `galleySystem` removes a spoiled serving, emits
   `mealSpoiled` and deposits `dirt.deposits.spoiledMeal` on its tile. Ration packs never spoil.
   With `spareServings: 1` one plate spoils per meal, which is the galley's steady trickle of dirt
   and the reason the cook mops it.
8. **Provisions are a stackable crate, bought like parts, through the parts code.** `item.provisions`
   (tag `provisions`, mass 2, `maxStack` 20; `galley.servingsPerCrate` 12), eighteen in the
   `pantry` zone at the start (six days for nine with waste). `provisionsLow { itemDefId, count }`
   (owner galley, public, routine; dedupe `provisionsLow`) is open while the servings aboard
   cover under `galley.lowBelowDays` (1) of `crewAboard × watchesPerDay`; `count` is the crates
   that bring it to `restockToDays` (4). Commerce derives the same `buyParts` task spec it derives
   for `partsNeeded` (one `buySpec` helper; target the galley compartment; label "buying
   provisions" from the item name) and `buyFromMarket` settles both kinds. No new task type, no
   new command: the narrator's `stockMarket` already stocks any item. Bought crates are set down
   at the top of the cargo ramp as parts are and the cook fetches from the nearest known stack;
   no haul to the pantry this slice. Every port market lists provisions.
9. **Items and tags.** `ITEM_TAGS` + `provisions`, `meal`; `TOOL_KINDS` + `mop`; `item.hot_meal`
   (`food`, `consumable`, `meal`; nutrition 0.4 against the ration's 0.25; mass 0.5; three meals
   a day at 0.4 keeps hunger near zero, so the call threshold decides who comes, not starvation)
   and `item.mop` (unique `tool`, kind `mop`), one in the `cleaning_locker` zone. The galley finds
   provisions and the meal def by tag, and L2 requires exactly one `meal`-tagged def and at least
   one `provisions`-tagged def, so no item id lives in config.
10. **Dirt is one quantised row-major grid per deck, in state, owned by `systems/dirt`.**
    `WorldState.dirt = { cells: Record<DeckDefId, number[]>, tiers: Record<LocalKey, DirtTier>,
    dustThisVisit: number }`, a cell an integer 0..255 (255 = 1.0) at `y × width + x` over the
    authored deck bounds (dock floor has no cell). 720 small integers on Mk2 are about 2.5 KB of
    JSON in a save; no typed array in state (CONTRACTS rule 2). `core/dirt-grid.ts` holds the
    index arithmetic and `emptyCells(deck)`, so `core/world.ts` and the migration can allocate
    without importing systems (the `core/watch-bill.ts` precedent). `Actor.boots: number` in
    [0, 1] is the only other state.
11. **Footfall and boots live in movement; everything else deposits through one function.**
    Spatial's `advanceActor` calls `trackFootfall(ctx, actor, geometry, from, to)`
    (`systems/dirt/footfall.ts`) when a segment completes: on a deck tile the tile gains
    `footfallPerStep` plus `boots × bootsShed`, boots lose that and gain `tile × bootsPickup`
    (after the shed); on a dock tile boots gain `grime × dockGrimePerStep` (the map's `grime`).
    A documented cross-system write, like the hazard scrape writing `scheduledFaults`. Every
    other source calls `depositDirt(state, content, config, deckId, tile, amount)` from
    `systems/dirt/deposit.ts` with the amount from `config.dirt.deposits`: `serving` at the
    serve, `eating` at the eater's tile, `cooking` at the stove, `spoiledMeal`, `repair` round
    the machine when a recipe completes (`equipment/recipes.ts`, pure over state so the recipe
    context suffices), `cargo` where a crate is set down (`steps.ts` `runPutDown`). Sparring and
    exercise deposits wait for slice 4's activities. Airborne dust is the dirt system's own: while
    docked at a map with `airborneDust > 0`, deck tiles within `airborneDustRadiusTiles` of an
    open ramp door gain `airborneDust × airborneDustPerDay` (falling linearly with distance) until
    `dustThisVisit` reaches `airborneDustVisitCap`, reset when the ship undocks. The cap is what
    keeps a dusty port from being a chore.
12. **Rooms are the unit of tiers, problems and thoughts.** `roomDirt = (1 − roomMaxWeight) ×
    mean + roomMaxWeight × max` over the room's floor cells; tiers `clean | untidy | dirty |
    filthy` at `dirt.tiers.{untidy, dirty, filthy}` with `tierHysteresis` on the way down, written
    to `dirt.tiers[key]` by `dirtSystem` (equipment phase, every `updateEverySeconds`) so a
    transition is seen once: `roomDirty` and `roomFilthy` (notable, ticker) rising, `roomTidied`
    (trace) falling, `roomCleaned { byActorId }` (notable) when a `compartmentDirty` resolves
    while someone holds its task. `roomFilthy` joins `host.slowDownOnEvents` (§9: a filthy-tier
    transition slows fast-forward). The "first field day" line is cut.
13. **Cleaning is a task with affinity, aging and a tier-driven class.** `compartmentDirty {
    compartmentId, key, tier }` (owner dirt, public, dedupe `dirty:<key>`) opens at `dirty`,
    resolves below `untidy`; `baseUrgency` is routine, essential while `filthy` and the room is
    tagged `galley` or `infirmary` (revision bumped on each tier change, so the task refreshes).
    It derives one `cleanCompartment` task: `requiredTools: ["mop"]`, target `{ kind:
    "compartment" }` (new `TaskTarget` variant), `interactionTiles` the room's `mopTilesPerTrip`
    dirtiest cells at or above half the `untidy` threshold, dirtiest first. `cleanPlan`: the mop
    (generic `supplySteps`: it is unique), each tile a `moveTo` and `mopSecondsPerTile` of `mop`
    work that sets the cell to `max(0, cell − mopPerTile × 255)`, then back to the cleaning
    locker and `putDown`. A trip that ends with the room still dirty releases the task and the
    next trip re-derives the tiles. Who: `cleanTaskAllowed` in `planner/cleaning.ts`, by the
    room's tags against the actor's roles (cook → galley, mess; deckhand → cargoHold, corridor,
    gym, airlock; engineer → engineering; medic → infirmary; captain, first officer, pilot →
    bridge; lounge, quarters, cabins and stores belong to nobody), anyone once the problem is
    `routineAgingDays` old, and anyone at essential class. Cleaning is routine and rounds are
    voluntary (slice 2 call 10), so **on-duty crew with nothing to do clean before they walk
    rounds, and off-duty crew never clean** (the off-duty rule rejects routine tasks); within
    routine class a dirty room competes with cargo on travel estimate, which the tier thresholds
    and the beats report keep honest (risk below).
14. **The renderer gets dirt on its own channel, at its own cadence, keyed by tick.** Not in the
    `RenderFrame`: frames come every 2 ticks and at 8× that is 80 a second; 720 numbers each
    would be most of the frame traffic for a picture that changes in minutes. `Kernel` gains
    `getDirtFrame(world): DirtFrame { tick, deckId, width, height, cells: Uint8Array }` and the
    worker host posts `{ type: "dirt", frame }` on load and whenever the tick crosses a multiple
    of `host.dirtFrameEverySeconds` (5); the main thread queues them by tick and applies the
    newest whose tick ≤ `viewTick` in the playback step, the way slice 2 queues slowdowns, so the
    future never leaks (D14). `interpolateFrame` is untouched. The canvas draws a translucent
    grime square per cell over the room fill, alpha `min(dirt.tintMaxAlpha, cell / 255 ×
    tintScale)`, skipping cells under 8: a path is visible as a darker lane, a room as a wash.
15. **The inspector learns rooms.** A tap on bare deck floor selects `{ kind: "room", id:
    compartmentKey }` (last in `pickAt`, after machines); the inspector asks for the snapshot at
    the view tick and shows the room's name, its tier as the subtitle, the mean and worst cell,
    and who is mopping it. Rooms come from `state.dirt.tiers` and `cells`, so the frame carries
    nothing new.
16. **Units.** Days (D76): `mealLeadDays`, `mealGraceDays`, `mealSpoilDays`,
    `substituteAfterDays`, `lowBelowDays`, `restockToDays`, `airborneDustPerDay`. Seconds:
    `cookSeconds` and `mopSecondsPerTile` (work at a machine or a tile), `updateEverySeconds`
    (a cadence of the sim, not of ship life) and `host.dirtFrameEverySeconds` (host real time).
    Per step or per event, unitless: footfall, boots, deposits.
17. **Schema 9 migrates, and gives an old save its stores.** `dirt` zeroed per deck in
    `deck.tiles`, `tiers` empty, `dustThisVisit` 0, `boots` 0, `galley: { lastMealWatch:
    watchIndexAt(tick) }`. A schema-8 save has no provisions and no mop; the migration spawns the
    scenario's `provisions` and `mop` starting lines into their zones as fresh items (ids from
    `nextId`), so a loaded game cooks tonight instead of eating rations until the next port, and
    says so in `warnings`. Narrator fixtures are regenerated, never hand-edited.
18. **Beats report is the tuning instrument.** `crew beats` (slice 2 W6) gains per watch: meals
    served, servings eaten hot and as rations, meals missed, servings spoiled, rooms at `dirty`
    and `filthy` at the end of the watch, rooms cleaned; per person the share of time `cooking`,
    `mopping` (beside `eating`); provisions days at the end. Targets for the tuning commit: the
    mess fills three times a day (at least six people eat within 0.05 day of each serve), a room
    reaches `filthy` at most once a day, mopping under a tenth of on-duty time, no meal missed
    with the cook aboard and awake.
19. **The digest says what the galley is doing and which rooms are dirty.** Ship section:
    `galley: 10 hot meals on the counter (good, Bettina Solheim), next meal due in ~90s;
    provisions 16 crates, about 5 days` and `dirt: galley dirty; cargo hold filthy` (only when
    some room is at `dirty` or worse); `provisionsLow` and `compartmentDirty` get problem lines
    (`provisionsLow` reuses `partsReason`). Mood lines already show the new thoughts' labels.
    Prompt v0.22 adds a galley-and-dirt paragraph: the cook's meals and a burnt dinner or a
    missed one are the crew's story to tell, provisions run out and are bought at ports
    (`stockMarket` may stock `item.provisions`), a filthy room is the ship's own failing; never
    invent food the sim has not cooked. `provisionsLow` and `roomFilthy` are narrative events (the
    digest sees them; they re-arm the quiet timer) and not hooked.
20. **The headline does not change.** Freight and the captain ashore already fill it; "buying
    provisions" shows on the buyer's label and in the ticker's `partsBought` line.
21. **Baking for fun and the galley-after-every-meal rule are not special-cased.** Baking is
    slice 4's recreation; the cook mops the galley because affinity sends her there, and the
    spoiled-plate trickle makes sure there is something to mop.

### Risks and hidden coupling

- **Hot files with one owner and gate stubs**: `planner/candidates.ts`, `systems/work/plans.ts`,
  `steps.ts`, `labels.ts`, `core/render.ts`, `systems/spatial/movement.ts`,
  `systems/equipment/recipes.ts`, `systems/commerce/tasks.ts`, `commerce/market.ts`,
  `work/derivation.ts`, `planner/helm.ts`, `apps/web/src/worker/host.ts`. The gate writes every
  call site and the streams reach them only through their own files (`systems/galley/*`,
  `systems/dirt/*`, `planner/galley.ts`, `planner/cleaning.ts`). A stream that finds it needs a
  line in another's file asks the lead, who adds it at integration.
- **Slice 2's streams are not merged while this is planned.** The gate assumes `whoHasTheHelm`,
  `helmRoleOf`, `gangwayHoldDone`, `gangwayCandidate`, `addThought` with the non-stacking
  refresh, `moodSystem` pruning, `crew beats` and `watch.nextOnWatch`'s inputs exist as the
  slice 2 plan and gate notes describe. If a name or signature moved at slice 2 integration,
  the gate follows the code, not this plan, and says so in its report.
- **Same-class commitment is the whole meal rhythm.** The call is routine; so are hauling,
  gangway watch and sleep. The plan relies on three things: the gangway hold ending for the
  call (`gangwayHoldDone`) with its candidate suppressed; the helm meal bypassing the planner;
  and haulers and sleepers simply eating later. If the beats report shows fewer than six at the
  counter per serve, the first knob is `mealCallHungerAbove`, not the urgency class: raising a
  meal to essential would pull people off cargo and the helm.
- **Cleaning competes with cargo inside routine class** on travel estimate, and a dirty room is
  always nearer than the dock. The tier thresholds make `dirty` rare (once a day per room), the
  affinity table keeps the deckhand on the hold and the cook on the galley, and the beats
  report's `mopping` share is the tripwire; if hauling starves, the lead raises `tiers.dirty`
  before touching urgency.
- **Rounds lose to cleaning by design** (voluntary against routine): the officer of the watch
  mops only an aged room, so Mara still walks her rounds, but Priya in a dirty engineering room
  mops first. A reader who expects engineers' rounds to continue through a dirty room is wrong
  until the room is clean. The beats report's `rounds` share says whether that is too much.
- **The provisions stack and `supplySteps`**: the generic supply step picks up a whole stack
  (`split: false`); the cook's plan splits one crate. The same whole-stack pickup is why
  `buyParts`'s crates are set down at the ramp rather than carried to the pantry; a cook
  fetching from the ramp walks the length of the ship, which the digest will show as a long
  "fetching provisions". A haul into the pantry is slice 4's if it grates.
- **The helm meal is a hold that eats**: `galley.helmMeal` is state (a save mid-meal resumes it),
  the serving within reach must still exist at the end (someone else may take it; then no

### Calls made

1. **Recreation is a need, not a problem, and it never goes above routine class.**
   `Needs.recreation` rises at `needs.recreationPerDay` (0.6: the seek threshold in a bit under
   a day). The planner's `recreationCandidate` (`planner/recreation.ts`) is offered off duty
   only: `routineObligation` above `seekThreshold`, `voluntaryActivity` above
   `recreation.joinAbove` (0.25, so a game fills the way the mess call fills), nothing below.
   `needUrgency` is not used for it: nobody leaves the helm or a crate to play cards, and the
   watch's relief rule (hunger, fatigue, comfort, social past `needEssentialAbove`) does not
   know the new key. On-duty crew with nothing to do still walk rounds (slice 2); recreation is
   what the off-going watch does. Restoring is per tick while the activity runs
   (`restores.<need> / durationDays` per day, from the def), so an interrupted game restores in
   proportion; `fatigueCost` is charged the same way. Needs reads this through one hook,
   `recreationRatesFor(ctx, actor)` in `systems/recreation/needs.ts`, called from

2. **One candidate per person: the best-scored activity, not one per activity.** Candidates in a
   class rank by travel estimate, which would make the nearest game the chosen one. So
   `recreationCandidate` scores every `ActivityDef` itself (interest: strong like 3, like 2,
   neutral 1, dislike 0; a dislike is never chosen; +1 for an open session with room, +0.5 for
   a session holding someone the actor likes (affinity > 0), −∞ for one holding someone at or
   below `social.avoidBelow` (−3); hosts with mood above `mood.highAbove` add 1 to group
   activities; mood below `mood.lowBelow` removes group activities altogether, solo only) and
   emits one candidate (`recreate:<activityId>`), ties broken by join over host over solo, then
   travel. Joining an open session needs score ≥ 1 and recreation above `joinAbove` (a neutral
   player makes up the table when they have time on their hands); hosting needs a like (score
   ≥ 2). The design's "score ≥ 2 to walk over" is kept for hosting and relaxed for joining so a
   two-person game is not a coin toss on who likes what.

   "join" | "solo"; sessionId?; ticksDone; ticksNeeded }`; a step kind, not a hold, because it
   carries the activity). On its first tick a `host` opens a session (or joins one already open
   for the activity at the venue), a `join` joins (full, gone or already running past
   `recreation.lateJoinSeconds`: fail `occupiedPosition`, backoff), a `solo` just starts

   step becomes `solo` with the def's `solo` variant, or fails; the others' steps fail), ends a
   contest or debate at once with no result when a member leaves and fewer than `min` remain,

   `abortActivity` so a bell, a meal call or an essential task takes the member out of the
   session the tick they leave; it also sets down a book or instrument they were holding.

## Slice 5: The asteroid field

Re-planned 2026-10-06 with the owner, after slice 4 landed (D89, D90). The slice first sequenced
here (engineering-console diagnostics, drills, a station framework with tactical and shields

Scope: the field as a narrator beat with the narrator in control of its shape and of chosen hits;
detection by the helm and a ship `alert` state; impacts uniform over the deck with blast radius;
machine effects (graze, damage, knockout, fault); a global hull bar, hull scars patched underway with kits
for part of their cost and fully repaired at port; getting stuck when the engines are out; ship
loss and game over; intercom panels and the reporting reflex; the officer of the watch holding
the bridge and sending scouts; rocks, impact flashes, scars, the hull bar and alert on the phone.
Nobody aboard is hurt this slice (bodies arrive in slice 6). Save schema 11 → 12 (migrated).
Narrator prompt v0.24 (`pnpm test:live` once).

Cut from the earlier slice 5: drills (owner: no muster drills); the medic, until injuries exist
(slice 6); `seedTopic` (no deck motion; later); engineering-console diagnostics (later); the
tactical/shields station and the station framework (slice 8).

Built as one serial gate commit, then five parallel workstreams, then a tuning commit by the
lead. Calls 1–17 are the design; 18–28 are the gate's, made when the slice was planned against
the code.

### Calls made

1. **The field is a narrator beat, not a route property.** A new narrator command
   `scheduleField` replaces the `asteroidBelt` hazard kind (removed from `HAZARD_KINDS`; the
   instant nav hazards `debrisField` and `ionStorm` stay the helm's). The route says nothing; the
   narrator decides where and when, so the same lane never plays the same twice. Budget: one
   field per leg, in the same window rules as `scheduleFault`/`scheduleHazard`.
2. **The narrator gets real control over the field** (owner: it must be able to tell a story,
   including predetermining outcomes). `scheduleField` carries:
   - `severity`: `light | heavy | brutal`, mapping in config to the mean impact rate, the share
     of large rocks and the field's length (90–180 s at 1× to start);
   - `detection`: `rolled` (the helm's check, call 4) or `unseen` (the field is noticed at the
     first impact, whatever the helm does);
   - `scripted`: up to three chosen impacts, each `{ atSeconds, target, rock?, effect? }` where
     `target` is a machine, a compartment or a tile, `rock` is `small | large`, and `effect` is
     one of the machine effects of call 7. A scripted impact replaces a random one at that
     moment, so the rate holds.
   - Targeting a person ("the hit injured Tomas") waits for bodies in slice 6, when `target`
     gains an actor; the command shape is ready for it, the kernel rejects it until then.
   - The kernel guard: scripted impacts alone may not take the hull to zero (validation drops
     the field with a reason the narrator sees). Random impacts can, which is the danger.
3. **Kernel field state.** `WorldState.field?: ActiveField { id; severity; startsAtTick;
   noticedAtTick?; endsAtProgress; impacts; stuckImpacts; scripted }`, from schedule to exit.
   The field occupies a stretch of the leg's route progress; the ship is in it while progress is
   inside that stretch. It ends when progress leaves it, or after `field.stuckDriftImpacts`
   impacts taken while not moving (call 9).
4. **Detection and alert.** When a `rolled` field is `field.warningSeconds` (12 s) from its start,
   the helm-stander rolls piloting against the severity, the same curve as nav hazards. Pass:
   `fieldSighted` ("Jax: rocks ahead"), the ship goes on alert and the impacts begin after the
   warning. Fail, an empty helm, or `unseen`: nothing, until the first impact sets alert. Alert
   is `WorldState.alert?: { sinceTick; cause: "field" }`, cleared `field.alertClearSeconds` (60 s)
   after the field ends. While on alert:
   - recreation ends and is not offered (voluntary loses to the essential work below anyway; the
     alert also closes open sessions);
   - sleepers sleep unless an impact lands in their compartment (they wake and see it);
   - the officer of the watch stays on the bridge (owner) and sends scouts (call 11);
   - damage problems raised by impacts are essential class.
5. **Impacts are uniform over the deck** (owner: every square the same chance; if the engines
   are rarely hit, make them bigger in content, not weight the dice). An impact picks a tile
   uniformly from the deck's hull footprint (walls included). Arrival is Poisson at the
   severity's rate; helm skill lowers the rate (call 8).
6. **Rocks have a size and a blast.** `small` hits one tile; `large` hits the tile and its eight
   neighbours (radius 1), the start of area effects that later weapons reuse. Every impact costs
   hull (`field.hullDamage.small`/`large`) once, however many tiles it covers, and leaves a scar
   on its centre tile (call 10).
7. **Machine effects are rolled, and one hit can take a system from whole to out** (owner: a
   single shot can knock a system offline). Every machine with a footprint tile inside the blast
   rolls once on `field.machineEffects`, a weighted table:
   - `none`: the rock glanced off;
   - `graze`: integrity −0.2;
   - `damage`: integrity −0.5, so a whole machine is derated (D35: below 0.5);
   - `knockout`: integrity to 0, inoperable from full in one hit;
   - `fault`: one of the machine's fault defs, through the existing fault path.
   Integrity damage is restored by the existing repair path (a damage repair raises the
   `machineFault`-style problem the equipment system already uses for blocked machines; the gate
   names it). Starting weights: none 25, graze 25, damage 25, knockout 10, fault 15. A machine
   with no faults rerolls `fault` as `damage`.
8. **The helm threads the field.** Rate × (1 − 0.4 × skill/10) with someone at the helm, ×1.5
   with nobody; the helm never changes damage. The helm's check is on detection (call 4), not on
   every rock.
9. **Stuck is a clock, not a cliff.** With the engines inoperable the ship stops making progress
   and stays in the field at ×0.7 the rate (tumbling with the rocks, not crossing them). After
   `field.stuckDriftImpacts` impacts while stuck, the ship drifts clear and the field ends. A
   stuck ship that fixes its engines resumes and leaves by progress. The question is how much
   hull is lost before the engineers finish, never whether it is infinite. Stationary is never
   safer than a manned helm underway, so cutting the engines is not the right play.
10. **A global hull bar, FTL-style** (owner; supersedes simulation-design §10.6 "no mandatory
    global hull-health bar"). `WorldState.hull { integrity; max; scars: HullScar[] }`, `max`
    from the hull class (100 for `light_freighter`). Each impact's hull damage is recorded on its
    scar. Underway, a scar is patched with a `patch_kit` from a locker: a `hullScar` problem
    (discovered; seen in the room), a `patchHull` task (fetch a kit, walk to the scar, weld,
    engineering ≥ 1) that restores `field.patchRecovers` (half) of that scar's damage and marks it
    patched. At port the crew repair the rest themselves (owner): if the port sells
    `hull_plating`, a `hullDamaged` problem buys it on the parts-buying pattern (D47, from ship's
    money) and `plateHull` tasks install a plate at each scar, restoring the rest of its damage
    and clearing it. Nothing gates departure on the hull: a conflict between repairs and something
    else (a departure, short money, no plating at this port) is the narrator's hook, not a check.
    Kits: two per locker zone across the ship (`patch_kit`, stackable), also bought at port.
11. **The officer of the watch holds the bridge and sends scouts** (owner). Every impact is felt
    by everyone aboard ("the ship shudders"): a `hullImpact` fact with no place, except that
    whoever is in the compartment sees where. The officer of the watch, on alert, raises a
    `compartmentToCheck` problem (public, essential) for each compartment nobody has reported
    since the last impact; the nearest available on-watch crew member takes it, walks there, and
    perception does the rest. The captain is the officer of the watch on their watch and is not
    sent.
12. **Intercom panels and the reporting reflex** (owner: comms in 5). A new machine,
    `machine.intercom` (tag `comm`, one wall tile, `operate` position), one per compartment on
    Mk2 and Mk1, placed in content. When someone learns of new damage (a scar, a damaged
    machine, a fault symptom) during alert, their next step is the nearest working panel,
    a 3 s `report`, which writes every damage fact they hold to everyone aboard as `report`
    evidence with the original observation time (the `tellCrew` pattern) and resolves that
    compartment's `compartmentToCheck`. Ticker: "Bettina on the intercom: galley's hit, stove's
    out." It is a reflex in the planner (containment-class, above the work it reports), not a
    weighed task, and it is not repeated for facts already public. A panel is a machine, so it can
    be hit: a reporter walks to the next one. The bridge has no automatic hull sensors: the
    player sees the hull bar fall; the crew learn where only by eyes and intercom.
13. **Ship loss is game over** (owner). Hull at zero: `shipLost` event, the run ends, the save
    is marked lost (`lostAtTick`), and the web shows an end screen with the ship's last day and a
    new-game button. No partial survival, no escape pods.
14. **No departure check on the hull** (owner). The digest tells the narrator the hull, the
    repairs under way and whether the ship is leaving with them unfinished; the prompt asks it to
    make that a story when it matters and not to schedule a field the hull cannot survive unless
    the ship left damaged.
15. **What the narrator sees.** Watch digest lines: field scheduled (severity, detection,
    scripted targets; "[scheduled; nobody aboard knows]"), field in progress (impacts so far,
    stuck or moving, who is at the helm), hull `integrity/max` with scars patched and unpatched
    and repairs under way at port,
    damage known to the crew against damage that exists ("the hold has a scar nobody has seen"),
    and who reported on the intercom. `hookOnEvents` gains `fieldSighted`, `fieldEntered` (unseen),
    `fieldCleared` and `shipStuck`.
16. **On the phone.** In transit, a rock layer between the stars and the hull and a sparser one
    over the ship (the foreground slot), both scrolling with the stars while a field is near or
    active; an impact is a short flash on the hit tiles; scars are a per-tile overlay on the dirt
    channel's pattern (a scar frame, applied at playback tick); the hull bar sits in the HUD
    beside the ship's state; alert tints the HUD and labels the bell line "ALERT". Auto-slowdown
    fires on `fieldSighted`, the first impact, `shipStuck` and any machine made inoperable.
17. **People are not hurt this slice.** An impact on a tile with someone on it hits the tile; the
    person gets a `nearMiss` thought (−) and a ticker line. Honest and visible, nothing pretended:
    no fake injury state before bodies exist.
18. **The hull footprint is every deck tile** (gate). Walls are edges in this deck model, not
    tiles (`DeckDef` doc), so "walls included" means no tile is excluded: every non-space
    character of `DeckDef.tiles`, machine footprints, doors' tiles and the tiles along the hull
    alike; dock floor never. `core/hull.ts` `hullFootprint(deck)` is the one list both the pick
    and the validator read; a large rock's blast is the centre and its eight neighbours clipped
    to the footprint (`blastTiles`).
19. **When and where a field lies** (gate). `scheduleField` carries `windowTicks`, bounded and
    sampled exactly as `scheduleHazard`'s (`failures` stream, `narrator.maxFaultWindowSeconds`).
    The sampled tick is the field's start (the first impacts may fall from then); the helm's
    check (call 4) falls `field.warningSeconds` before it. A start that falls before transit
    waits for the first `transit` tick, like a hazard; the field then occupies
    `severities[s].lengthSeconds × travel.nominalSpeed` of route progress from where the ship
    is, clamped to the leg's end. One field per leg: the command is rejected while
    `state.field` is present, and an ended field stays (phase `ended`) until the leg ends, when
    the field system drops it; a field whose leg ends before it starts is dropped with
    `fieldMissed` (notable, no ticker line), never carried. The same preconditions as a hazard
    (a course planned or under way, transit still ahead), and none while the hull is lost.
20. **Scripted impacts, exactly** (gate). Each is `{ atSeconds, target, rock?, effect? }`:
    `atSeconds` counts sim seconds from the field's start and must be below the severity's
    `lengthSeconds`; at most `field.maxScripted` (3); `target` is `{ kind: "machine" }`,
    `{ kind: "compartment" }` (a tile of it, uniformly), `{ kind: "tile" }` (in the footprint) or
    `{ kind: "actor" }`, which the kernel rejects with "people are not targets until bodies
    (slice 6)"; an absent `rock` is drawn by the severity's `largeShare`; an absent `effect` is
    rolled. The guard prices an absent `rock` as `large`: if the scripted impacts' hull damage is
    at or above `hull.integrity`, the command is rejected (`preconditionFailed`, "scripted impacts
    alone would sink the ship"), at validation and again at apply. A scripted impact the field
    never reaches (it drifted clear first, call 9) is dropped silently: the digest lists what
    fired.
21. **Integrity damage has its own repair** (gate). The design left "the `machineFault`-style
    problem" to be named: it is `machineDamaged { machineId }` (owner `damage`, `discovered`,
    essential, dedupe `machineDamaged:<id>`, seen in the room like any machine problem), deriving
    one `repairDamage` task (engineering ≥ 1, a `welder`, `damage.repairSecondsPerIntegrity` ×
    the integrity missing, standing at a `repair` position) that restores integrity to 1 and
    resolves the problem. It is independent of faults: a `fault` effect takes the fault path
    (D6) and its repair is the fault's recipe. Only impacts raise it (W2's `damageMachine`);
    overheat keeps D35 (its fault repair restores integrity), so a game with no field is
    unchanged.
22. **Kits and plates by tag, welded** (gate). Two item tags, `patchKit` (`item.patch_kit`,
    stackable 10, 2 kg) and `plating` (`item.hull_plating`, stackable 5, 12 kg); the sim finds them
    by tag, never by def id. `patchHull` and `plateHull` both need engineering ≥ 1 and the
    `welder` tool kind (the welder torch in stores) and consume one kit / one plate at the commit
    point; `damage.patchSeconds` 20, `damage.plateSeconds` 40. "Two per locker zone" is every
    `locker`-tagged zone (the twelve bunk lockers on Mk2: two kits each, 24 aboard); Mk1 has no
    locker zone and no scenario, so it carries none. Kits are sold at Crossroads Hub and Ossory
    Station; plating at all six ports, which all sell spare parts (Vesper Gate's line at count 0:
    "out of plating" is the narrator's to use).
23. **`hullDamaged` reuses the parts purchase** (gate). Owner `damage`; raised while docked at a
    port whose market lists `plating` and some scar is unplated with fewer plates aboard than
    unplated scars; payload `{ itemDefId, count }`; routine; derives commerce's `buyParts` task
    through `buySpec` (exported from `systems/commerce/tasks.ts` at the gate), target the market,
    so the purchase, the counter walk and the stowing are D47/D63's unchanged.
24. **A lost ship halts the kernel** (gate). `WorldState.hull.lostAtTick` is set (with
    `shipLost`) the tick integrity reaches 0; from then `advance` returns `{ kind: "lost" }`
    without stepping (a new `AdvanceHalt`), the worker posts `shipLost { tick }` once, and the
    CLI's pacing and beats runs stop. Nothing un-loses a ship; a save of a lost run restores lost.
25. **Scars ride the render frame** (gate). Call 16's "scar frame on the dirt channel's pattern"

    at playback tick), not a second worker channel: scars are a handful of entries and
    `RenderFrame` is already applied at playback tick, so `RenderFrame.hull.scars` carries them
    and the overlay cannot lag the flash. Impacts reach the frame from the tick's ticker events
    (`asteroidImpact` is notable), so no state holds them.
26. **The alert record and who writes it** (gate). `WorldState.alert { sinceTick; cause:
    "field"; clearsAtTick?; reports: Record<CompartmentId, Tick> }`: the field system (W1) raises
    it and sets `clearsAtTick` when the field ends, the intercom (W3) writes `reports[c]` when a
    report covers compartment `c`, and damage response (W2) reads `reports` against the field's
    `lastImpactTick` to raise and resolve `compartmentToCheck`. Alert is common knowledge (the
    klaxon): `AgentView.alert()`.
27. **The event vocabulary** (gate). Field (W1): `fieldScheduled` (trace), `fieldMissed`
    (notable, hidden), `fieldSighted` (narrative), `fieldEntered` (the first impact landed:
    narrative when nobody had noticed the field, notable when the helm had), `asteroidImpact`
    (notable), `machineKnockedOut` (notable), `nearMiss` (notable), `alertRaised` and
    `alertCleared` (notable), `shipStuck` (narrative), `shipUnstuck` (notable), `fieldCleared`
    (narrative), `shipLost` (narrative). Damage (W2): `scoutSent`, `hullPatched`, `hullPlated`,

    `narrator.hookOnEvents` gains `fieldSighted`, `fieldEntered`, `fieldCleared`, `shipStuck`
    (a hook fires only on a narrative occurrence, so a sighted field's `fieldEntered` does not
    hook twice).

D-entry text it proposes, and what it saw in a headless run. The split is the brief's five, by

### Risks and hidden coupling

- **Hot files with one owner and gate stubs**: `planner/candidates.ts`,
  `systems/work/plans.ts`, `steps.ts`, `actions.ts`, `labels.ts`, `core/render.ts`,
  `view/agentView.ts`, `core/world.ts`, `core/migrations.ts`, `core/invariants.ts`,
  `systems/commerce/tasks.ts`, `apps/web/src/worker/registry.ts`, `apps/cli/src/pacing.ts`. The
  streams reach them only through their own files (`systems/field/*`, `systems/damage/*`,
  `systems/intercom/*`, `planner/alert.ts`, `planner/intercom.ts`, `core/hull.ts`). A stream that
  needs a line in another's file asks the lead.
- **W1 calls into W2 at two points, and only there**: `damageMachine(ctx, machine, loss)` for
  every graze, damage and knockout, and `onImpact(ctx, impact)` once per impact after the hull
  and the scar are written. The gate's stubs lower integrity and do nothing, so W1's tests see
  machines go dark with no problem raised until W2 merges; W2's tests drive `onImpact` by hand.
  Neither stream emits the other's events.
- **The hull's arithmetic has three writers** (W1 charges, W2 patches and plates), so the
  invariant (`integrity = max − Σ(damage − recovered)`) is the contract: every write goes through
  `core/hull.ts` helpers, a charge is clamped at zero and recorded as what was actually lost, and
  plating removes the scar in the same step that raises the integrity.
- **Alert has three readers and one writer.** W1 raises and clears it; W2's behaviour and W3's
  reflex read `state.alert` (sim) or `view.alert()` (planner). `alert.reports` is W3's to write
  and W2's to read. A field that ends while scouts are walking leaves `compartmentToCheck` open
  until the alert clears; W2 resolves every open check when it does.
- **Essential work on alert against the watch.** Damage problems are essential, which respects
  the watch (D77): the off-watch sleep on unless the impact is in their room, which is the design
  (call 4). If the beats show scars unpatched for a watch, the fix is the alert calling all hands
  (rating patching containment while on alert), not lowering the off-duty rule.
- **The report reflex is containment class**, above essential work, so a cook mid-repair walks to
  the panel first. The reflex must end (the facts it carries become public at the broadcast);
  W3's test that a second witness does not report again is the guard against a loop.
- **Stuck and travel's drift.** Travel already stops progress when propulsion is lost
  (`RenderTravel.drifting`); the field reads that and never writes travel. The approach hook
  (`stageDock`) holds transit at the leg's end: a field clamped to the leg's end must end before
  the hold, or the ship sits in rocks waiting for the narrator. W1 ends the field at the
  approach lead (`narrator.approachHookLeadSeconds`) at the latest.
- **`machineDamaged` and the departure gate.** D36's gate asks `isMachineBlocked`; a knocked-out
  engine holds departure for repairs like a faulted one, which is wanted. A damaged (derated,
  not blocked) machine does not.
- **The intercom placements are content the validator checks** (floor, compartment, reachability,
  off spawn points). Sixteen new machines on Mk2 shift machine instance ids (`m34`…): any test
  that pins a machine id by number is a literal to fix, and the narrator fixtures are regenerated.
- **Ship loss and the hosts.** `{ kind: "lost" }` is a new `AdvanceHalt`; every `switch` on
  `halt.kind` must follow (the worker host, pacing, beats, test fixtures that loop on `advance`).
  A lost save restores lost and halts at once: the web's load path must show the end screen,
  not a frozen ship.
- **Counts and literals pinned in tests**: machines 20 → 21, items 18 → 20, thoughts 26 → 27;
  `contracts.test.ts` narrator authority; `capabilities.test.ts` "six levers" (W5 makes it
  seven); the hazard kind lists in narrator tests.
- **Narrator call volume**: four hooked types, at most one field per leg, so at most three hooks
  a field (sighted or entered, stuck, cleared). `asteroidImpact` is notable and reaches the
  ticker and the uploaded events; at a brutal field's eighteen impacts it is the loudest source,
  which the tuning commit holds.
- **Slow tests under a loaded runner.** On a 16-core machine the full parallel run already timed
  out three or four of the slowest real-content tests now and then before the gate (the galley,
  harmonica and passenger runs, each a few seconds alone); sixteen more machines and 24 more items
  make every real-content tick a little dearer. A stream that adds real-content runs gives them the
  explicit `{ timeout }` `fullSim.test.ts` uses rather than leaning on vitest's 5 s.
- **Save size**: a scar is about 150 bytes; a brutal field leaves twenty, plated away at port.
  `field` is one row; `alert.reports` at most sixteen keys.
